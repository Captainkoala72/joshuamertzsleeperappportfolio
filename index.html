Round 1Pick 1

With the first overall pick in my project draft…

A full 3D tennis game with a real career mode, built by one person and a lot of AI.

Tennis is my favorite sport, so I built the tennis game I always wanted. DeuceTennis started as a 2D prototype in C# WinForms (physics, scoring rules and AI opponents), then I rebuilt it in 3D in Godot. The goal from day one: make it feel like a 2K or Madden game, with a dev team of one.

I run it like a studio where AI fills the roles. Claude Code and Codex write and refactor the code, Meshy builds the players, ElevenLabs voices the commentators, umpire and crowd, Google Flow helps with video, and Blender, driven by 10,000+ lines of Python tooling, builds the courts, trophies and everything in between.

My favorite detail for this application: there's a social network inside the game. BallBuzz has three sports networks and five analysts reacting to your matches, and you choose how to post back: humble, hype, cocky or trash talk. Your fan meter rides on it. I've already built a tennis social feed. Now I want to run a real one.

05The Vault

Before it was my major, it was my weekends.

Minecraft UHC, 2014 – 2016. The origin story.

UHC (Ultra Hardcore) is a Minecraft mode where your health doesn't regenerate and one death sends you home. Every match needs a host to schedule it, post it, set up the server and the scenarios, and referee the chaos live. From December 2014 to July 2016, that host was me.

As xVex, I ran a little over 50 games for 40–80 players at a time across The Arcus Network, Arcus UHC, Neighborhood and Apollo UHC. I rented and self-hosted servers, configured plugins (and wrote my own, MeleeFun, in Java), designed graphics, promoted every match on Reddit and moderated all of it. Thousands of hours. It's the reason I got into event management.
